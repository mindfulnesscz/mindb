# Access levels and gated delivery

Two questions look like one, and treating them as one is what caused the problem this system exists
to fix:

- **Discovery** — who can see that an asset exists, and obtain its URL. Postgres row-level security.
- **Delivery** — who can fetch the bytes at that URL. A Cloudflare Worker in front of R2.

Until 2026-07-31 only the first was enforced. R2 objects sat on a public bucket domain, so a URL was
a bearer token: `perm` decided who could *find* a file, and nothing decided who could *open* one.

This page describes both halves, and the machinery that keeps them agreeing.

## The four levels

| Level | Who | Bytes served by |
|---|---|---|
| `public` | anyone with the URL | the public bucket, directly — no Worker, no cookie |
| `guest` | anyone signed in, including a magic-link visitor with no client | the Worker |
| `client` | members of that client, plus staff | the Worker |
| `internal` | staff only — `editor`, `admin`, `super_admin` | the Worker |

`client` is what the pipeline creates assets at. Anything world-readable is promoted deliberately,
one asset at a time.

## Effective level: `perm` AND `status`

`perm` says who may see an asset; `status` says where it is in its lifecycle. Both gate:

```text
effective_level = (status in ('approved','published')) ? perm : 'internal'
```

An asset marked `public` while still in `draft` is staff-only. That is what stops unapproved work
being published by a stray dropdown — and when this shipped it immediately closed **245 production
assets** that were `perm=public` with an unreleased status, and therefore world-readable.

`assets.effective_level` is a **stored generated column**, so Postgres and the Worker cannot
disagree about it. It is not writable: set `perm` or `status` and it follows.

## Two buckets

| Tier | Bucket | Hostname | Public access |
|---|---|---|---|
| public | `dc-hub-bucket` | `cdn.disruptcollective.com` | yes, by design |
| gated | `dc-hub-gated` | `files.disruptcollective.com` | **none** — the Worker's binding is its only door |

Two buckets rather than one bucket with a path exclusion, so the failure mode is **closed**: if the
Worker or its route breaks, gated objects 404. With a single bucket behind a public custom domain,
the same mistake publishes the whole library.

The gated bucket has no `r2.dev` URL and no custom domain of its own. Attaching one would undo the
tier in a single click — that is the one setting to never change.

## The key IS the authorization

```text
files.disruptcollective.com/{level}/{client_id}/thumbnails/{stable_id}/{child_id}.webp
                            ^^^^^^^ ^^^^^^^^^^^
```

The Worker reads the level and the client straight out of the key and decides from the signed
cookie alone — no database call, no KV read, nothing awaited but the R2 body. A 50-tile gallery
costs 50 signature verifications rather than 50 lookups, which is the difference between a tool
that browses and one that does not.

Two consequences, and both are load-bearing:

- **Changing a level moves the bytes.** That is bought deliberately. See the next section.
- **A key the Worker cannot parse is a 404**, not a fallback. Legacy objects with no client segment
  had to have one inserted during the rollout for exactly this reason. Anything writing a gated key
  validates it against the Worker's shape first.

Public keys are unchanged from what the pipeline has always written, so an asset that is genuinely
public keeps the address it already had and never has to move.

### Three namespaces

```text
{level}/{client_id}/thumbnails/{stable_id}/{child_id}.webp        one per asset
{level}/{client_id}/originals/{stable_id}/{child_id}{ext}         one per asset
{level}/{client_id}/pages/{stable_id}/{child_id}/001.webp         one per PAGE of a document
```

`pages` holds per-page previews of PDFs, decks and Word documents for the portal's page viewer. They
are **derived** bytes and carry the level of the document they came from — a `client` deck whose pages
sat under a public key would publish the deck's content just as surely as the deck itself would.

A directory per asset, not a flat `{child_id}-001.webp`, so every page of one document shares a prefix.
That is what makes "remove the pages this document no longer has" a prefix listing rather than a guess.

**There is no page URL column.** One object per page makes fifty URLs per asset absurd, so the portal
derives each address from the asset's `thumbnail_url` — same domain, same level, one segment different
(`pageUrlsFromThumbnail`). The pages therefore inherit whatever tier and level the thumbnail resolved
to and cannot disagree with it.

## Keeping keys and levels in step

This is the part that is easy to underestimate. Changing `perm` or `status` updates a row instantly
— discovery is correct immediately — but the object still sits at the old level's key until
something moves it.

```text
perm/status change            portal edit · gallery cascade · pipeline · hand-run SQL
        │
        ▼  trigger on public.assets  (fires only when effective_level actually changes)
  cdn_move_queue              durable, needs no credentials
        │
        ▼  drained by whoever is already authenticated
  cdn-reconcile               edge function, beside the data
        │
        ▼
  bytes moved to the key the level requires
```

### Page previews are moved by LISTING, and their source is deleted

Both movers — `cdn-reconcile` and `scripts/rekey-gated-objects.mjs` — find objects by reading the
`thumbnail_url` and `download_url` **columns**. Page objects appear in no column, so they were
invisible to both: narrowing a deck from `client` to `internal` moved its thumbnail and left its pages
readable under the old `client/` prefix. Both now additionally LIST each of the four level prefixes for
the asset's page directory.

One deliberate asymmetry. For a thumbnail or original the reconciler **leaves the source in place** —
its URL column has been repointed, so the move remains recoverable. The next desktop upload that
touches that identity confirms the current target and then prunes its thumbnail and original from all
other levels, unless another live row still references the key. For a page the source is **deleted as
part of the move**, because there is no column to repoint: the old object would sit at a wider level,
reachable by anyone holding a cookie for that level. Orphaned is not the same as unreachable.

The desktop sweep is deliberately per-asset. It cannot find bytes for a row hard-deleted outside the
normal flow or for a client whose desktop never runs again. The manual bucket-wide collector closes
that residue with the parent R2 credential:

```bash
node scripts/gc-cdn-objects.mjs --env staging                 # report only
node scripts/gc-cdn-objects.mjs --env staging --execute       # report, confirm, delete
```

It lists both buckets, protects every canonical and stored row reference plus non-asset namespaces,
and writes the complete plan before deletion. It is not part of the desktop sweep and never touches
Cloudflare Stream. See [CDN garbage collection](/operations/cdn-garbage-collection) for the report,
flags, confirmations, and safety rails.

**A trigger, not a call from the portal**, because the portal is not the only writer — and the two
it cannot see are the ones most easily forgotten: the gallery-inheritance cascade rewrites
children's `perm` with no portal involved, and the pipeline flips `status` when a file leaves or
returns to disk.

**A queue, not `pg_net`**, because having Postgres call the function needs its URL and a service
token stored where the database can read them — another secret at rest, for something entirely
routine. A queue needs no credentials, and it is durable where fire-and-forget is not.

**Drained by its callers**: the portal after an edit, the desktop at the end of a run. Both are
best-effort and neither can fail — a failed call delays a move rather than losing it, because the
row stays queued.

An empty `cdn_move_queue` means bytes and levels agree. To check, or to drain by hand:

```sql
select count(*) from public.cdn_move_queue;
```

There is also a manual escape hatch for bulk work — a fresh environment, a one-off re-key:

```bash
node scripts/rekey-gated-objects.mjs production            # dry run, changes nothing
node scripts/rekey-gated-objects.mjs production --execute
```

It is idempotent and copies before it deletes. `--delete-source` is a separate, later pass and is
the only irreversible step.

> **There was briefly a GitHub Actions cron doing this.** It was removed: a core correctness
> property should not depend on a CI provider, a checked-out branch, and secrets pasted into a
> settings page — and it ran the script from whatever was on `main`, so a fix reached production
> only when someone merged. Nothing in the running system reads from git.

## The cookie

Signing in mints a short-lived signed cookie (`dch_cdn`, ~30 min) scoped to
`.disruptcollective.com`, so the portal's `<img>` requests carry it to the CDN subdomain
automatically. The portal renews it at three quarters of its life.

It is minted by the **Worker**, not by a Supabase edge function, for a reason worth knowing: a
cookie's `Domain` must be a parent of the host that *sets* it, and a response from `*.supabase.co`
cannot set one for `.disruptcollective.com`.

URLs are permanent; the **session** expires, not the link. A leaked URL keeps working and simply
returns 403 to anyone not signed in. Presigned URLs were considered and rejected — they would make
every stored `thumbnail_url` a ticking clock.

Credentials are sent **only** to the gated host. The public buckets answer with
`Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, and a browser rejects a wildcard origin on a credentialed
request — so sending them everywhere silently breaks every download, the only symptom being that
the save dialog becomes a new tab.

## Galleries and variants

A **gallery** is one deliverable stored as many rows, so its images inherit the parent's level —
enforced by a database trigger, not by whichever code path happens to write. Setting a gallery
child's level individually has no effect; the portal shows it read-only. To show part of a shoot
more widely, split it into its own gallery folder.

A **variant set** is different: separating a rendition's visibility is a real if rare need (an
internal print master beside a public web version), so the portal offers a checkbox, on by default.

## Managing a level

### Who may change one

`perm` is **portal-owned**. The pipeline supplies it once at INSERT and then has no opinion —
`stripPortalOwnedFields` drops it from every update. That is deliberate: before it existed, every
publish run silently undid an editor's promotion or lock-down, and because the level is in the object
key it dragged the bytes back too.

`status` is different and still written by the pipeline on updates, because that is how a row whose
file returned to disk stops being `disconnected`.

Changing a level requires `editor`, `admin` or `super_admin`. A member can never change one; RLS
gives them no update rights on `assets` at all.

### What happens when you change one

One edit sets off four things, in this order:

1. **Discovery changes immediately.** RLS re-evaluates on the next query, so the asset appears or
   disappears from galleries at once.
2. **A trigger queues the asset** (`cdn_move_queue`), but only if `effective_level` actually
   changed — `perm` and `status` are written on most pipeline runs, and queueing every asset every
   run would turn a queue into a full-table sweep.
3. **`cdn-reconcile` moves the bytes** to the key the new level requires, and updates
   `thumbnail_url` / `download_url`. The move is a copy: the original stays until a separate
   `--delete-source` pass removes it, which is what makes the whole change reversible by repointing
   the URLs back.
4. **If the asset is a video, the same pass flips Stream's `requireSignedURLs`** — see
   [Video on Cloudflare Stream](/cloud-storage/video). A video's level lives in that flag exactly as
   an image's lives in its key, so a `public` → `client` demotion that skipped this would keep
   serving to anyone holding the uid.

Steps 3 and 4 need a caller to drain the queue. The portal does it after an edit and the desktop
after a run, so in practice it is seconds — but an empty `cdn_move_queue` is the only proof that
bytes and levels agree.

### Changing many at once

There is no bulk control in the portal, on purpose — "make these 300 assets public" is not an action
worth making easy. For a genuine bulk change, edit `perm` in SQL and let the trigger and reconciler
follow:

```sql
-- Queueing is automatic; verify before and after.
select effective_level, count(*) from public.assets group by 1 order by 1;
update public.assets set perm = 'client'
 where client_id = '…' and perm = 'public' and parent_id is null;
select count(*) from public.cdn_move_queue;   -- should be non-zero, then drain to zero
```

Gallery children are omitted (`parent_id is null`) because the inheritance trigger sets them from the
parent — including them would fight it.

`scripts/rekey-gated-objects.mjs` is the tool for the object side. `--reclassify-public` exists but
is never run by a timer: it changes who can *see* things, which is a decision.

### Verifying it took

| Question | How |
|---|---|
| do bytes and levels agree? | `select count(*) from public.cdn_move_queue` — zero means yes |
| what is world-readable right now? | `supabase/audit/perm_exposure.sql` |
| any family disagreeing internally? | `supabase/audit/perm_family_mismatch.sql` |
| is a specific URL actually refused? | fetch it signed out — expect 403, not 404 |

A 404 where you expected 403 means the object is not where its row claims, which is drift rather than
a permission problem.

## What this does not do

**It does not un-publish anything.** A URL that has already been served stays fetchable at that
address forever by anyone who kept it. Re-keying gives objects new addresses and limits future
exposure; it cannot undo past exposure. Everything published before 2026-07-31 — **1006 distinct
production objects** — should be treated as permanently public.

**Gated content does not load on Vercel preview deployments.** A cookie scoped to
`.disruptcollective.com` is never sent to `*.vercel.app`, and `SameSite=None` would walk into
third-party-cookie blocking. Test gated delivery on `staging.hub.disruptcollective.com`.

**The move is not instantaneous.** It is seconds, not milliseconds, and only when a caller drains
the queue. Discovery is immediate regardless, so the window applies only to someone already holding
the URL — the leaked-URL case that re-keying cannot address anyway.

Four programs must agree on the key shape: the pipeline writes keys, the reconciler and the re-key
script move them, and the Worker parses them back to decide access. **All four import
`assetStorage.ts`** — including the Worker, since `workers/cdn-gate` is an npm workspace member and
wrangler resolves `@sotto/domain` through it at bundle time.

That was not always true. The Worker carried its own copy of the parser on the assumption that a
separate deployment could not share code, and `parseObjectPath` exists as one function because that
assumption turned out to be wrong. There is now no second place to change the key format, which
matters more than it sounds: a half-made change to that shape is a 404 on a file the portal is
offering, or bytes served to someone who should not have them.
